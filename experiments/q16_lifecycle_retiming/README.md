# Q16 — Retiming the register lifecycle

Is the register's **depth** causal, or only its presence? Can the `v*`-aligned register
state be induced before it naturally forms, or after it dissolves — and if it can, does it
do the same job?

## What the pilot showed (FLUX.1-dev, one prompt, one seed — to be replicated)

- Removal keeps the natural v\* state out of every block that would read it; the writer
  rebuilds it only at the writer blocks and the next hook strips it; the clean sinks go
  and attention spreads out rather than moving.
- Whether a v\*-aligned token is a sink is set by the block reading it: single-stream
  blocks make it one at about the natural level wherever it came from; dual-stream blocks
  do not. Every block reads its input through a LayerNorm, so a token's norm sets how long
  the state lasts, not whether it is a sink.
- A state written before formation is not a sink where it is written, survives into the
  natural window, and the writer then skips those tokens and writes others.
- A state maintained past the natural end lasts to the output; the natural dissolution is
  done by a few blocks (about 34–40), not by late depth in general.

**Set `INTERVENTION_STEPS` before reading any image.** It decides which *denoising* steps
the edits fire at, and it is the dose rather than a nuisance parameter. The first pilot
ran one step out of twenty — 24 hook calls where 480 were available — and its images came
back visually identical to clean, while the sham was *exactly* clean and every real arm
was non-zero and rank-ordered by hook count. That is a dose-response at a dose too small
to see, not a null result about the register. `'all'` (the default) is what an
image-level readout needs; `'capture'` is the one-step mechanistic readout and is not
evidence about images.

## The Q1/Q4 revision

Before any image difference is read, the run itself has to show two things:

- the natural `v*` state was genuinely suppressed;
- the early or late replacement was genuinely established.

`EXPERIMENT_DESIGN.md` §0 says which changes Q1 and Q4 directly support and which remain
Q16 hypotheses. In short:

- **Suppression** is subtractive, dynamic over every image token at every hook, and
  judged against the paired clean run at the same step and block. The norm-preserving
  operator is kept as an operator control (`F_norm_preserving`). It cannot test sink
  identity: both models read every token through a LayerNorm, so it hands the next block
  the same input as the subtractive operator (see `MAIN_RUN.md`).
- **Four separate results replace COMPLETE/INCOMPLETE (cell 5.3):**
  - (a) was the natural `v*` state kept out of every consuming block, the unhooked
    boundary block included? This is measured on the residual each block receives.
  - (b) did the original carriers come back, and did new carriers appear, new relative
    to clean?
  - (c) did attention stay on the original sinks, or redistribute, head by head?
  - (d) were the early/late states written at every hook, at every step?
- **Windows** sit on the measured lifecycle (FLUX.1-dev):
  - early 14–16;
  - one suppression schedule for D, E and F, at 17–39 plus a cleanup at 40;
  - C maintained from 28, before the decline, and extended through 40–42;
  - D's replacement at 41–43, after the cleanup.
- **Site pilot (4.5).** Block-input induction is compared with a transfer into the
  feed-forward's input from the natural writer block, and with only its `v*` part (Q4).
- **Image comparisons.** Only the comparisons 5.3 marks interpretable are read as depth
  results: E vs F, D vs F, and C vs A.

## How a state is induced

**Primary: natural-register-matched.** At every block of the window and every edited
denoising step, each carrier gets the clean natural register's `v*` projection **and** its
norm. Both are expressed relative to that block's median (`induce_matched`,
`calibrate_register_match`). The reference is the natural register on its plateau at the
same denoising step, so only depth moves. 5.2 verifies both numbers at every hook. 6.7
measures alignment, norm, sink behaviour and persistence block by block.

**Control: direction-only.** The earlier lower-strength injection runs as a
`<key>__dironly` twin of B, C, D and E, with the same schedule. It sets the `v*` projection
only and leaves the token near ordinary norm.

**Matching two statistics is not transplanting the register.** The rest of the token's
direction, anything beyond `v*`, and its history are not matched. 4.2 measures the first
against an ordinary-token baseline. It also reports, per block, whether the matched state
is unusually large for the recipient, and it never clips the target.

## The four levels, and why they are separate

| level | measured by |
|---|---|
| **L1** a `v*`-aligned state exists at the target depth | carrier projection vs the clean **p99 of every token at those blocks** |
| **L2** it is also high-norm and lasts the window | carrier norm vs `highnorm_ratio × median` |
| **L3** it draws attention with favourable query–key geometry | absolute sink strength, `qk_cosine` |
| **L4** downstream computation and the image change | LPIPS, detail metrics |

Alongside them, the results that judge whether the intended states exist:

| measurement | what it answers |
|---|---|
| **(a) received state** | did any consuming block, or the first unhooked block after the interval, receive a token more `v*`-aligned than every ordinary token of the paired clean run? |
| **(b) regrowth / relocation** | did the original carriers come back, and did the state appear at new positions? New is judged against the paired clean run. Reported; gates nothing |
| **(c) attention** | per head: did the clean sink persist, or where did attention go? A remaining non-register sink does not contradict (a) |
| **(d) induction** | was the intended state written at every hook of its window, at every step? |
| **continuity** | did NATURAL + LATE extend one lifetime, or create a second state after a gap? |
| *consumer cosine* | *secondary only (6.4): reported with how many blocks it could score* |

The secondary consumer view is measured at `InterventionPoint.ATTENTION_INPUT` — **the tensor the image
self-attention is called with**, after the adaptive norm and after its scale and shift.
That site matters: FLUX applies the modulation inside `AdaLayerNormZero`, so the norm
module's output *is* what the QKV projection reads, while PixArt applies it in the block
body, so the module output is *not*. The norm divides token magnitude out and the
modulation can put some back, so whether the consumer sees magnitude is measured per run
(`consumed_norm_spread`) rather than assumed. Where it is ~1 the computation receives a
**direction**, and delivery is scored as alignment with `v*` against the clean run's
*ordinary* ceiling at that same block.

**L4 without L3 is a perturbation with a visible effect, not a retimed register.** The
verdict cell in 7.3 will say `PARTIAL: ... Do NOT describe the lifecycle as retimed` in
exactly that case, and it fires the stopping rule when L1 is never reached.

Persistence is classified from the measured trajectory as **maintained** / **carried** /
**reconstructed**, and returns `not measured` when no unpatched block follows the window.

## What the run refuses to do

- proceed with no high-norm, `v*`-aligned carrier at the natural window;
- invent an early window where none can exist (a writer range starting at block 0) — the
  conditions that need it are **skipped and reported**, not approximated at another depth;
- treat a run whose induction did not happen as a lifecycle shift — 5.2 raises instead;
- call suppression achieved on the strength of the rule going quiet. Every interval
  carries one **terminal cleanup** hook past its end. The block after that cleanup is
  measured on what it actually receives, and a leak there is reported as its own result;
- let D, E and F use different suppression schedules, or let any block carry both a
  suppression hook and an induction hook, where the two would cancel. 4.4 hard-stops on
  either;
- use a removal rule that touches more than `RULE_MAX_SHARE_OF_IMAGE` of the image at any
  hook of the clean run;
- read an image comparison as a depth result when 5.3 has not shown the states removed and
  established;
- withhold an image because the induced state never became a sink. That dissociation is a
  result, and every condition's image is generated, saved and labelled;
- clip an out-of-distribution target coefficient. It is reported, with the ratio to the
  largest projection any token reaches at that depth.

## Reproduce Stages 1–3

```bash
python -m pytest tests/test_lifecycle.py tests/test_lifecycle_delivery_e2e.py -q  # 131 passing
```

The decisive one is `test_the_suppression_is_bit_identical_to_the_validated_beta_gamma_operator`:
conditions D, E and F only compare if all three suppress identically, so the lifecycle
suppression is asserted equal to `control_surface.control_surface_states(β=0, γ=1)` rather
than assumed to match it.

And the two in `test_lifecycle_delivery_e2e.py` that check the delivery site against the
**running model** rather than the diffusers source:
`test_what_the_attention_receives_is_measured_at_the_attention` (the residual the hook
handed the block is > 3× spread; the tensor the attention is called with is < 1.5×), and
`test_the_norm_module_output_is_the_attention_input_only_on_flux`, which requires those two
tensors to be **identical on FLUX** and to **differ on PixArt**. The first build probed the
norm module's output and so measured a pre-modulation tensor on PixArt — cosines off by up
to 0.16. A diffusers change in either direction now fails a test rather than changing a
result.

Verified offline end to end on `tiny-flux1` and `tiny-pixart`:

- the sham reproduces clean at every probed block;
- every suppression schedule closes its terminal boundary;
- the operator writes its target, and for the matched arms the projection **and** the
  norm, per token;
- the absolute attention scale is used throughout.

`test_the_matched_state_is_exactly_what_each_maintained_block_receives` checks, through the
real hooks on both families, that what each maintained block's computation receives
carries the target projection and norm.

## Known confound, reported not corrected

On FLUX.1 the early window lands in **dual-stream** blocks and the late window in
**single-stream** blocks. Any early-vs-late difference there is confounded with the
attention architecture. Cell 3.3 detects this and prints it; 7.3 repeats it in the
verdict. **PixArt-Σ is uniform single-stream and is the cleaner test of timing.**

Early-window carrier positions are **oracle-selected** from a later clean block — valid
for mechanistic diagnosis, not a deployable procedure.
